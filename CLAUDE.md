# Castle Defense: Medieval — project handoff

A medieval village-builder + tower-defence browser game prototype (inspired by Kingdom Rush and Age of Empires). Owner: Jack. He tests mostly on his phone (portrait and landscape) and also on desktop.

The game ships as one file, `index.html` (~880 KB, with three.js r128 beside it in `vendor/three.min.js`, and `sw.js` for offline play), **built from `src/`** (see Source layout): edit `src/`, never `index.html` by hand. It was built over ~37 iterations in a claude.ai chat and published as an artifact; this repo continues it in Claude Code (300+ commits since).

## Style rules (from Jack)
- Tips, tutorial lines and anything that explains how to do something are short plain phrases with no "·" or full stop (Jack): "Tap the barracks, then Rally to send soldiers to a spot", "Tip: build towers where raiders come in"; the "·" stays only in labels and log lines.
- **Storyline** (Jack): only on the back of the campaign's stage cards, never in the game (no story cards, banners or summary lines). The player, a young heir, wins back the kingdom from the Warlord, who took it and killed the King, their father; **gender neutral** (Jack): never "prince", "princess", "boy" or "King" for the player ("you escaped as a child", "the true heir", "your first summer on the throne", "your town"); the Raider Chief helped him; the bonus stages are the new King's hard years. Light in tone, not mysterious. No short forms in story text: "will", "is", "do not", never "'ll", "'s", "don't" (Jack). About 60 words a stage so it fits a phone's card.
- Words for resources (Jack): things on the map are "trees" and "rocks"; what you collect is "wood" and "stone", as on the top bar ("No trees nearby", "No rocks nearby"); never "forest"; a counted amount is plural, "Mine 120 stones" (Jack).
- UI text is minimal: a few words, no explanations (hints like "Pick a spot", "Occupied", "Need [wood]5"; banners "Night 2 · 8 raiders"; log lines "<b>Cottage</b> ready"). Keep new text in this style.
- Colour code: town = blue (flags, soldiers, tier-3 roofs), raiders = red, villagers = yellows. Roofs by tier: tier 1 straw thatch (`M.thatchOld`: strands radiate from the peak by object-space angle, level course lines by world y), tier 2 compact rounded logs from ridge to eave like the palisade (`logRoof`: `M.thatch` for pyramids: staggered wooden shingles (rows by world height); `M.logGable` for gable slabs by local x; each has its own `customProgramCacheKey`), tier 3 blue tiles (`M.roofBlue`, `roofGrit`). Every roof has a thick eave: `pyramid()` and `coneRoof()` (blue tower cones) return a group with the roof plus a band under its edge; gable slabs are 0.1 m thick. Bases by tier: tier 1 house, lumber camp, quarry, castle, barracks and archer tower stand on a packed-earth slab (`M.court` with the `earth` grit: mottled packed earth with dark and pale pebble specks, like the palisade fort's yard; `SLAB`, top `SLAB_TOP` .08, added in `makeModel`) that fills the footprint, so the fort's palisade sits on it and neighbours join up; tier 2 bases are plain grey stone (`M.plainStone`, rock grit, no block joints); tier 3 bases keep the masonry `M.stone`. `plinthTop(type, tier)` gives the top height for `snapToNeighbours` (placement, within 0.8 m) and `stoneAt` (paths stop at any base's edge); Bases (footprint-wide meshes below ground level) take one shade per tier in `makeModel`'s tinting, so neighbours match. `planFlatten` may lower ground on an edge shared with a locked neighbour down to the new building's level (never raise it), so no base gets buried; the T1 slab is 1 m deep. `flushHeight` finds the matching height, and `settleBase` re-levels a building after each upgrade so its new base stays flush with neighbours whatever their tier (only within 0.3 m; much higher or lower ground is left alone). Tier 1 farms have no base.
- Medieval look over clean/modern: crooked timbers, daub, thatch, small shuttered windows.

## Recent model notes
- Phones: the long loops load a compressed `.m4a` copy first (`afconvert -f m4af -d aac -b 96000 x.mp3 x.m4a`), falling back to the `.mp3`; regenerate the copy when a loop is replaced. Night/bird loops don't preload. The top panel has no backdrop blur, and fog follows zoom and sits well back (`near = dist + 100`, `far = dist + 240`; Jack found the old dist + 35/130 made the top of a portrait screen look blurry) so the top of a portrait screen stays crisp. The renderer runs at the `devicePixelRatio` up to 2 on High (Jack: at 3 his iPhone got hot; it was 2, then 3 because 2 looked soft, then 2 again: a side-by-side at phone size showed no difference, and 3 draws 2.25 times the pixels); `adaptResolution` (in `frame`) averages frame time every 3 s and drops `pixelK` by 0.5 (floor 1.5) while frames average over 24 ms.
- **Riders queue at gates** (Jack: horses seemed to pass through the wall beside a gate): a rider (`v.cav`) heading through a gate waits 1.3–2.6 m out while anyone is within 1.2 m of its middle (`gateWait`, in `updateSoldier`), so they go through one at a time; only outside the opening, so two never wait on each other. `unstick` never hops corner-wise past two blocked sides, and its hop is drawn as a jump (`v.rx/rz` set), not eased across the wall.
- `separateUnits` (after unit updates each frame) pushes villagers and live raiders apart (radius .24/.26, horse .55); fallen raiders are fixed obstacles; no push into unwalkable ground.
- Juice: `shake(amp, at, kind, gap)` (building hit .05 at most once per 1.5 s, destroyed .3 at most once per .6 s (`shakeNext`; a big raid used to keep the screen shaking non-stop), a running bigger shake is never cut short by a smaller one; fades with distance from the camera focus, applied around render only), `debrisBurst` + `updateDebris` on raid destruction, raiders flash (`hitFlashM`, `flashT`) when hit, puff dust as they fall (`hurtEnemy`) and sink away by 1.9 s; health bars (raiders, soldiers, buildings) show only once damaged (a building's bar and its build-progress bar float at `barTop(B)`: `BAR_TOP` for the older types, else measured from the model's bounding box per tier once it stands finished; the newer buildings had no `BAR_TOP` entry, so their bars sat at NaN and never showed), finished/upgraded buildings bounce in x/z (`B.pop`) with a dust ring, `coinFly` sends DOM coins from on-screen gold payouts to `#cGold` (pulse), villagers cheer (hop, arms up) for 2.6 s at dawn (`cheerT`). `Wave_horn.mp3` plays at nightfall (`SND.horn`).
- **One-time tips** (`tipOnce(key, html)`, `updateTips`; seen keys in `localStorage['mtd-tips']`): the first time a mechanic matters, one line in a pill with a thin 1 px gold edge whose glow pulses (`tipGlow`, 2.6 s, up to an 18 px glow; Jack found the 2 px rim too heavy, then the first soft glow too faint) above the dock (and the night powers) for 6 s, one at a time: the raider marks (from Day 2, or any day once the guide is off), the arrow volley and the riders (first nightfall; while each one's tip is up its round button pulses, bigger and brighter, `tipPulse`, `.pw.tipPulse`: Jack; the riders' tip says "a horseman", two with Second rider), Rally (first barracks or stables), Move (first catapult), trading (first market), decor's boosts (first decor), Skip day's gold (fourth building; not on Stage 1), Upgrade (Stage 3). **Low food** (Jack) is the one tip shown once every game, not once ever (`foodTip`, `foodTipDone`): the first time by day the food chip turns red (`res.food` below what the hungry need), outside the tutorial, "Food is low, build more **Fields**" (or **Farmhouses**, whichever the town has), adding "or **Upgrade** them" once one stands below the stage's top tier. Building names, abilities and actions are bold in every tip, guide line and defeat tip (Jack): "Tap the **Barracks**, then **Rally** to send soldiers to a spot", "Tap the **Market** to **Trade**", "Use the **Arrow volley** at night".
- **Guide spot**: during the guided day's build steps soft gold tiles (`guideTiles`, `tileSpot`, `updateGuideSpot`; one per cell of the building's footprint, inset .12 m so the gaps read like the grid, opacity pulsing .1–.3: Jack wanted a grid, not a disc, and less bright) mark a good open spot for that step's building: houses beside the castle, fields to its other side, lumber two thirds of the way to the nearest woods, archers 7 m out toward the night's first raider mark (`findValid` from there); they are rebuilt whenever the step changes (the old disc stayed where the house had been built).
- Guided first day (`GUIDE`, `guideTick` from the HUD tick, `guideMark` adds `.guidePulse` to the tab/tool to press): house → lumber camp → farm → archer tower → upgrade; steps tick off by game state, Skip button; once done/skipped `localStorage['mtd-guide']='done'`. Each step is a short line with the building in bold (Jack: more than the bare names, but not long), `applyLoadout`'s `STEP`: "Build a House for more villagers", "Build a Lumber near trees for wood" ("Build a Forester for wood"), "Build Fields for food" ("Build a Farmhouse for food"), "Build an Archers to guard the town" (Jack; it read "a Archers" and "a Fields") ("Build a Crossbow to guard the town"), "Tap a building to Upgrade it" (Jack's wording, building names in bold; Stage 1 has no upgrade step, so on Stage 3, which opens tier 2, the same line is a one-time tip, `tipOnce('upgrade')` in `startGame`); the guide pill shows them as HTML (`#gText` innerHTML).
- Horses (`mountHorse`) have knees: each leg is an upper leg from the hip and a lower leg with the hoof on a knee pivot (`userData.knee`, `fore`); `horseGait(legs, phase, amp)` gallops them (each leg on its own beat, `GALLOP_OFF`: hind left, hind right, fore left, fore right; while a leg swings forward the foreleg's knee folds the lower leg back, the hind hock folds it forward), used by raiders, the stables' riders and the gallery. Stable horses (`stallHorse`) only idle.
- Roof flags are all one size (`roofFlag(parent, x, y, z, k)`: a 1 m pole from y, a .45 × .3 flag at its top; k undoes a scaled parent): archer towers, barracks, stables, crossbow towers. The castle keeps its own bigger flags.
- Walls connect only to walls and gates (`WALLY`), never to towers or the castle.
- Ground shadows: `syncTerrain` must call `tGeo.computeVertexNormals()`; r128's shadow lookup reads the normal even with no normal bias, and without normals the terrain received no shadows at all.
- Grit: `addGrit(material, 'wood'|'stone'|'rock')` adds world-space noise (wood grain, stone mottling, rock specks) on top of any existing shader, with its own `customProgramCacheKey` (needed: three keys programs by onBeforeCompile source, identical for every grit kind); `tinted` copies the key.
- Units are drawn at `UNIT_K` (.78) so they fit doors; enemy HP bar height uses it too. `unstick` kicks in after 2 s stuck.
- At dawn defenders come out of their post: archers and crossbowmen out of the tower's or castle's door (`v.indoor` = the post), in their own clothes; a catapult's crew (who are the villagers themselves, see the catapult) step off the engine's tile and change back on the spot (`v.crewStrip`, `updateCrewStrip`).
- Archer towers, crossbow towers and ballistas turn like other buildings (`FACING`) and are reached from the back (`DEF.back`: `doorCell` takes the tile behind; `DOOR` points are on the model's −z): tiers 1–2 of the towers and every ballista have a ladder up the back (archer T1 rough logs, T2 timber past the parapet; crossbow T1/T2 likewise; ballista T1/T2 timber, T3 stone rungs), tier-3 towers a door there (`DOOR_M[3]`; the stone archer tower's shaft is turned π/8 so a flat face is at the back, and its door sits back in a stone frame of jambs and a lintel over a dark reveal; the stone crossbow tower's door has the same frame). At nightfall defenders walk to the tile behind, step to the ladder's foot (`innerDoor`) and climb (`v.climb`, `updateClimb`: hand over hand at .9 m/s up to the post height `climbH`, drawn by `updateClimb`, which the pose loop skips) before they appear at their post (`showDefender`; `manned` only once they are up); at a door they go in (`goIn`, the doorWalk's `on`). At dawn they come down one at a time (`wait` .a × 1.4 s) and walk out to the tile behind, or come out of the door (`exitDoor`); dawn mid-climb sends them back down.
- Braces: the archer lookout and the ballista's T1/T2 stages carry a diagonal brace on all four sides, cut to run post to post (`braces`).
- Defender posts (`POSTS`): castle, tower, crossbow (fighting top), ballista (on its deck), catapult (on the ground: loader at the winch, gunner behind); `showDefender` dresses each by the building's tier (`armour`) with a bow, a crossbow, or nothing for siege crews. Ghosts for every tool are made at the end of setup (stables and siege models use materials defined late).
- Arrows leave from the bow (`bowPoint`: raises the bow arm, reads the bow's world position) for tower/castle archers and raider archers.
- Construction: `addScaffold` builds poles every ~1 m, plank decks per lift, braces, a ladder and material stacks; walls/torches/flowers get a light version (`LIGHT_SCAFFOLD`). Builders kick up dust (`dustAt`, shares the smoke `puffs`).
- Walls are not flattened and have no AO plane; their posts/blocks run 0.3–0.6 m below ground so runs follow slopes cleanly.
- Every building with a door or a front is in `FACING` (the mine, stables, forester, farmhouse and the newer decor were missing: no Rotate button, and they were placed facing front even onto the map's edge); a type not in `FACING` is only ever checked at face 0 in `evaluate`, and the card's Rotate skips facings outside `DEF.faces` (none use it now).
- A door never faces the map's edge (`doorOk`: the tile in front of the door and the next one the way it faces are both on the map, `inMap`), so smart rotate turns a building at the edge away from it (villagers used to walk out over the edge); a door along the edge, facing sideways, is fine (Jack: the old rule refused the whole outer ring of cells, so a building on the edge row could only face inward).
- Doors and facing: `DOOR[type][tier]` is each model's door in its own frame (front = +z). Every building except walls, gates and paths can face any side (`FACING`, `B.face` 0–3 = +z/+x/−z/−x, `faceRot`/`unrot`): `placeFace` is set with Rotate / R while placing (ghost turns with it); smart rotate: if that facing's door tile is blocked, `evaluate` picks the nearest turn whose door is open (order +0, −1, +1, +2 quarter turns; result in `ev.face`, used by the ghost and `place`), and the card's Rotate turns a placed one to the next side whose door tile is open (`doorOk`), else "Door blocked". `evaluate` rejects a spot whose door tile is blocked. Posts (`postOffset`) and farm obstacles (`farmBlocked`) follow the face. `doorCell` lines the door tile up with the door (1×1 and 3×3 on the centre, 2×2 tier-1 doors at local x −1). A door may sit off the tile's centre line (lumber shack T1 at x −0.65, the hut nearer the middle; lumber camp T2/T3 laid out like the shack: the cabin where the shack's hut stands (`LODGE_AT` −.55, −.15), drawn at `LODGE_K` .7 in x/z and `LODGE_KY` .8 in height, its door full size on the shack's door line (`LODGE_DOOR` x −.65, z .66), window right of it; log pile front right (T3 four high), chopping stump back right (1.15, −1.35); T2 a staked stack of chopped firewood on the cabin's right, T3 the sawbuck there): `doorShift` (measured from the tile's centre, 1 m off a 2×2's middle) moves the door point and the path tile's arm into the building across to it. The forester is the exception (`doorShift` returns no shift): its way in is the gate in the middle of its fence, and shifting toward its hut door at the back left (x −1.6) drew a stray path stub beside the gate, into the grove, on any path laid in front of it (Jack, twice). No paths are laid automatically: the player builds them. Villagers walk from the door tile straight to `innerDoor(B)` and only vanish there (`goIn`/`exitDoor`/`doorStep`, `v.doorWalk` pauses their logic); loads are dropped once inside (`dropLoad`).
- Opening doors: `leaf(w, h, t, m, hx, y, z, dir, open)` builds a door leaf hung on a hinge (`userData.leaf` = swing angle). The castle (T1 hall door swings out over a dark doorway; T2/T3 double gatehouse doors swing in) and gates (two leaves, closed across the opening, swing out) open while a visible villager is within 1.7 m of the door (`updateDoors`: castle at `innerDoor`, gate at its centre; `B.doorK` eases open 6/s, shut 2.5/s). Raiders never open them.
- Archers draw with both hands at the bow for `DRAW` (.45 s) before each shot (`bowPose`: draw / loose / idle), town and raider alike. Melee raiders run (×1.6, faster stride, lean) within 6 m of their target.

## Technical notes
- **Colour**: `THREE.Color` setHex/setStyle/setHSL patched to convert sRGB→linear; renderer uses sRGBEncoding + ACES filmic, exposure 1.1. Overlays use `toneMapped:false`.
- **Terrain**: heightmap, 1 m vertex spacing (V=57). Placing a building snaps to cells, checks slope, flattens with smoothstep falloff, locks the footprint, lays a dirt splat, adds a plinth and clears trees. "Adapt terrain" is always on.
- **Auto-tiling**: bitmask N=1 E=2 S=4 W=8 for paths and walls/gates (`wallMask`, `refreshWalls`, `swapModel`).
- **Wall/gate rotation**: `B.rot` (0 = E–W, 1 = N–S) from `placeRot` (Rotate button in the dock / R while the wall or gate tool is active; ghosts rebuilt via `makeGhost`). A lone wall (mask 0) is a straight run along `rot`; connected walls auto-tile as before. A gate follows walls on one axis, else `rot`; rotating from the card (`rotateBuilding`, also R with it selected) sets `rotFixed` so it keeps that direction. Passability is unchanged (gates pass villagers either way).
- **Paths**: look by map (`PATH_STYLE`, `pathStyle()`: top colour, worn-edge `roadMatBase` colour, set stones, kerb): packed brown earth on the green maps, pale chalky gravel on Watchhill (brown earth looked wrong on its golden grass), grey scree on Mountain pass. Packed-earth tracks built from overlapping discs (centre + 3 per connected arm, radius wobbling by world position so neighbours agree): darker, wider base layer (`#4e3f2a`, radius .5) for a defined worn edge, smooth position-based colour on top, flat set stones, and a kerb of small stones along the outer edge (`rebuildRoads`: points on each base disc's rim outside every other disc, a few gaps by hash; all in `pathStones`, `MAX_PSTONES` N·N·24) (`pathStones` InstancedMesh; `frustumCulled=false` and `instanceColor` pre-sized, both needed in r128).
- **Pathfinding**: A* on cells (`findPath`, `cellPass`, `walkTo`), `clearLine` line-of-sight shortcuts, `unstick` after 3.5 s without progress, cache invalidated on any build/path change. `steer`/`turnTo` commit to one side round obstacles. Villagers enter/exit buildings via their door.
- **Speed of the game's step** (the full check, 2026-11: Warlord's Hold's night 7 with 80 raiders stuttered up to 207 ms a step): `cellPass` reads the cells nobody may ever enter (scenery, Watchhill's ruin, closed ground) from `cellOpen`, worked out once per map (the closed ground's noise was most of every route search); raiders' walking-distance fields (`landField`, for `reachTarget`) are kept per start cell in `fieldCache` over a mode-3 pass mask `pass3` (buildings count as open there, so they only change with the ground); `cacheCells` clears all three, and so does the end of `startGame` once the stage's scenery and camps stand; raiders re-pick their target every 2.6–3.4 s (it was exactly 3 s, so a column re-picked in one step). Now about 4 ms a step on average, 45 ms at worst, in headless Chromium.
- **GPU memory** (the full check: about 750 shapes leaked for every stage started and 300 for every 20 raiders, so a long phone session kept growing): three keeps a geometry's buffers until it is disposed, and raiders, upgraded buildings' old models and whole maps were only taken out of the scene. Every geometry three uploads is noted (`liveGeo`, by wrapping `BufferGeometry.prototype.addEventListener`, which three calls once per upload), and `sweepGeo()` disposes those no longer in `scene` or the gallery's `gScene`, at the end of `startGame`, at every dawn (`startDay`) and on leaving the gallery; one shown again is simply uploaded again. Restarting five stages three times now holds at about 450 geometries (it was 11,000) and the JS heap at 68 MB (92).
- **Fewer draw calls** (a second full check, Jack: make it more efficient): every building was 30–90 separate boxes, each a draw call and another for its shadow, and Warlord's Hold's fort 640 on its own. `mergeStatic(root)` (`02-scene.js`) joins every still mesh of one material into one, in the root's frame: at the end of `makeModel` (after the tinting, so the six shades per material stay), for the boss forts right after `darkFort` (their crews, camp raiders and ruins are added after) and for every bridge. ## Gotchas fixed before (don't reintroduce)
- `var paused` (not `let`) — TDZ crash on startup.
- Anything `showMapPick` (or anything else run during startup) touches must exist before the script reaches it: `hushSfx` read a `const liveSfx` declared further down, the startup `showMapPick` threw, the rest of the script never ran and the Campaign button was dead (Jack). It is a `var` now and `hushSfx` returns while the audio state isn't set up. Test the plain `index.html` (no `?debug`/`?map=`) after touching startup paths: `tools/render/serve.js` waits for `G`, which only `?debug` defines.
- No two faces at the same height in one model: the arbalest tower's shaft and platform slab shared a top face and the platform flickered between greys as the camera turned (z-fighting); keep stacked boxes' faces a centimetre or more apart (the great ballista's shaft stops .02 short of its slab).
- `crowns.instanceColor` is pre-sized to `MAXT`: r128 sizes it from `.count` at the first `setColorAt`, so switching to a map with more trees showed garbage crown colours.
- Global `[hidden]{display:none!important}` — otherwise flex modals ignore `hidden`.
- Restart confirm needs `resetArmed && ...` guard.
- Flag waves must anchor on position, not uv (uv tore between box faces).
- `randomNear` targets must not land inside a building footprint (villagers stalled at the castle gate).
- `evaluate` rejects solid buildings on another building's door cell (villagers exiting got snapped back into a blocked door forever). Flowers/torch/gate are still allowed.
- A `//` comment written onto the end of a line of code swallows everything after it on that line: four times now a python patch appended a comment to a line and silently cut off code that followed (the farm barn's door, the raider ballista's `m.add(t)`, Stonebridge's rock calls, and `recolor`'s snow and building dirt patches, which stopped being drawn). Put comments on their own line, or check the line ends where you expect.
- The file must keep `<!doctype html>`, `<meta charset>` and the viewport meta (the claude.ai artifact wrapper used to add them; without them phones render zoomed-out and arrows show as mojibake).
